# Inaya: long-term strategy

How Inaya grows from a new account into a character that earns. `docs/PLAN.md` covers how
content is produced; this covers what she posts, in what order, and how it pays.

## 1. Positioning

**A Karachi girl living a stylish, everyday life, who loves cars, so detailing studios hire
her.**

- **The feed is her life around the city:** cafés, food streets, the seafront, malls,
  rooftops, and selfies in her white Civic. It's relatable and easy to follow, with no
  storyline to keep up.
- **Cars are her interest, not her plot.** They show up in car selfies, at car meets, and
  in paid studio gigs.
- **Why it can work:**
  - Lifestyle content grows a broad audience.
  - The car interest gives her a niche that pays: detailing and PPF studios need
    polished content and new customers.
  - An AI character can post every day at high quality and low cost, once her reference
    set is locked.

## 2. What she posts

The mix, rhythm and caption voice are in `.claude/skills/inaya-brand-voice/SKILL.md`.

| Share | Pillar | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| About 60% | Going out: cafés, places, food, outfits | Builds a broad, loyal audience |
| About 25% | In and around her everyday car | Relatable, and keeps the car interest visible |
| About 15% | Studio gigs, car meets, auto shows | Earns money and proves her value to partners |

**A "life" without a storyline:**
- Recurring places (her café, her seafront spot).
- The same everyday car.
- One outfit per day.
- Real seasons and holidays.

That continuity is enough for followers to feel they know her.

## 3. Growth stages and money

Move on when the numbers say so, not the calendar.

| Stage | Followers (guide) | Focus | Revenue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Launch | 0–5k | Lock her identity, post daily, find her best-performing places and formats | Barter: 1–2 studios give access to their location in exchange for content they can repost |
| 2 · Studio gigs | 5k–25k | Regular paid studio visits | Paid gigs, monthly content packages, commission on bookings through a discount code |
| 3 · Brands | 25k–100k | Lifestyle and car brands | Cafés and restaurants, fashion, car-care, tyre and auto-app brands; monthly retainers |
| 4 · Own products | 100k+ | Products and licensing | Merch, co-branded products, platform payouts where eligible, licensing |

### The fastest earner: studio content packages

This doesn't need a big following. **Sell detailing and PPF studios a monthly package:**
for example, 2 gig visits a month giving 12 images and 4 Reels of Inaya at their studio.
- The kit's review presets and real-place prompts already make these.
- The studio posts the content on its own account as well, so it's worth paying for even
  while her following is small, and each post shows her to a new audience.

**Her lifestyle side opens a second market:** cafés, restaurants and clothing brands in
Karachi.

### Pricing: how to set it, not made-up numbers

- Talk to 3–5 studios, and compare with local micro-influencers and the cost of a
  studio's own photo shoot.
- Sell monthly packages, not single posts. Include usage rights: how long the partner may
  repost, and where.
- Discount the first 2–3 partners in exchange for case studies. Raise rates at each stage.
- Send every partner a short monthly report: reach, saves, profile visits, code uses.

### Media kit (build at stage 2)

One page covering:
- Who Inaya is, and the audience (age, city, gender split).
- Average reach and engagement.
- What a gig includes.
- Two or three case studies.
- The AI disclosure.

## 4. What to measure

| Stage | Watch |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Posting consistency, the QA pass rate, saves and shares, follower growth per week, which places and formats perform best |
| 2 | Studio enquiries, partner repost reach, code uses |
| 3 | Reach on sponsored posts vs organic, renewals |
| 4 | Product sales, licensing, revenue per post |

## 5. What the system needs

| Need | Kit feature (build stage) |
| --- | --- |
| Daily posts without repeats | Larger library: places, poses, selfie poses, hairstyles, outfits; randomizer with rules (stages 2–3) |
| A believable everyday life | Day planner with continuity, holidays and seasons (stage 4) |
| Captions in her voice | English–Urdu caption engine (stage 4) |
| Studio gigs | Gig mode: a studio brief turned into a full shot list and captions (stage 4) |
| Proof for partners | Posting log with results, monthly partner report (stage 5) |
| Scale | Automated image generation with your own API key (optional, stage 6) |

## 6. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Platforms limit or label AI content | Disclose openly and use the labels |
| "She's not real" backlash | Say it in the bio; the content's appeal is the point |
| Studios unsure about AI | Lead with case studies and reports, and offer exclusivity per area |
| Relying on one platform | Instagram first, then TikTok and YouTube Shorts |
| Legal and ethical | Paid-partnership labels, attributed claims, written permission for places and products; check local advertising rules |
| Identity drift at volume | The QA gate in `docs/PLAN.md`; always attach the reference images |

## 7. The first 12 months

| Months | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| 0–1 | References locked, system built, first 2 weeks of content banked, account launched |
| 1–3 | Daily posting, 1–2 barter studios, first case studies |
| 3–6 | Paid studio packages, media kit, first café or fashion collaboration |
| 6–9 | Regular gigs, first brand retainer, TikTok and Shorts added |
| 9–12 | Brand partnerships, product idea tested with the audience |
